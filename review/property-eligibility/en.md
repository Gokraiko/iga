# How to Check if a Property Qualifies for Turkish Citizenship Before Buying

**A property advertised for USD 400,000 is not automatically eligible for Turkish citizenship.** Before paying a deposit or signing a binding purchase agreement, foreign investors should verify the property's legal ownership, seller history, qualifying investment value, official documentation and payment structure.

A property may be legally available for purchase yet fail to satisfy the requirements for Turkish citizenship by investment.

For an investor planning to commit USD 400,000 or more, discovering an eligibility problem after purchasing the property can be expensive and difficult to resolve.

This guide explains seven essential checks to make before purchasing real estate in Türkiye for citizenship purposes.

## Quick Answer: How Do You Know Whether a Property Qualifies?

Under the Turkish citizenship by investment framework, qualifying real estate acquisitions generally require an investment of at least USD 400,000 or its equivalent, supported by the required transaction evidence and a three-year restriction against resale.

However, the investment amount is only one part of the assessment.

Before buying, investors should verify:

1. The property's legal title and ownership status.
2. The seller's identity and relevant ownership history.
3. The official investment value and required valuation documentation.
4. The banking records and foreign-exchange documentation.
5. The property's legal restrictions, mortgages and encumbrances.
6. The three-year holding commitment and citizenship-related title deed procedures.
7. Whether the complete transaction can satisfy the competent authorities' conformity requirements.

**A real estate agent's assurance that a property is "citizenship eligible" is not a substitute for independent legal verification.**

## 1. Check the Title Deed Before Paying a Deposit

The first step is to examine the property's official land registry records.

In Türkiye, the title deed is commonly called a *Tapu*. It provides important information about the registered property and its ownership.

A legal review should examine:

- The registered owner's identity.
- The property's province, district, parcel and independent unit information.
- Whether the seller has the legal authority to transfer ownership.
- Existing mortgages, liens, attachments or restrictions.
- Annotations affecting ownership or transfer.
- Whether the registered property corresponds to the apartment or commercial unit being advertised.

Investors should also consider building permits, occupancy documentation, zoning compliance and relevant condominium records.

A newly constructed apartment, a luxury development or a property with a ready title deed is not necessarily eligible for citizenship.

**Practical example**

An investor finds an apartment in Istanbul advertised for USD 420,000.

The developer confirms that the apartment has a title deed and can be transferred immediately.

However, the land registry review identifies an existing restriction or unresolved ownership issue.

The investor may need the seller to resolve the problem before proceeding.

**What to do:** Ask an independent lawyer to review the current official land registry records before making a binding financial commitment.

## 2. Verify the Seller and Previous Ownership History

A property's previous ownership and the identity of the current seller may affect its eligibility for Turkish citizenship by investment.

Certain transaction structures, previous transfers, related-party arrangements and restrictions involving foreign ownership require particular attention.

Investors should not assume that every property previously owned by a foreign national is automatically disqualified. Equally, they should not assume that every property being sold by a Turkish citizen is eligible.

The applicable rules, transaction dates, seller relationships and relevant ownership history must be examined together.

### Questions to ask before buying

- Who currently owns the property?
- Is the seller an individual or a company?
- Has the property previously been transferred to foreign nationals?
- Are the buyer and seller related or otherwise connected?
- Has the property previously been involved in a citizenship investment transaction?
- Are there restrictions arising from earlier transfers?

**Practical example**

Two apartments in the same residential development are offered at similar prices.

One has a straightforward ownership history.

The other has been transferred several times, including transactions involving foreign owners.

The apartments may require different legal assessments even though their location, price and physical condition are almost identical.

**What to do:** Request a transaction-specific ownership history review before relying on any seller's citizenship eligibility claim.

## 3. Confirm the Official Qualifying Investment Value

One of the most common misunderstandings is that the advertised property price alone determines citizenship eligibility.

It does not.

The Turkish citizenship by investment real estate route generally requires at least USD 400,000 in qualifying investment value.

The competent authorities examine the relevant official transaction values, payment evidence and required valuation or investment amount confirmation documentation.

Under the applicable Turkish Land Registry and Cadastre framework, the *Taşınmaz Edinim Sureti ile Vatandaşlık Kazanımına Esas Tutar Tespit Belgesi* (TTB) is an important document for confirming the investment amount in citizenship-related real estate transactions.

The official sale price and qualifying payments must meet the applicable threshold, with the relevant amounts assessed under the official rules.

### Example: A property advertised for USD 400,000

Imagine a seller advertises an apartment for USD 400,000.

The investor should not assume that this advertised price is sufficient.

The legal review must examine whether the transaction can be supported by the official sale documentation, banking records and applicable investment amount confirmation process.

If the relevant documentation does not establish the required qualifying investment, the transaction may not support a citizenship application.

### What if the appraisal is higher than the purchase price?

Suppose a property is being sold for USD 350,000 while a valuation-related document indicates a value of USD 420,000.

The higher valuation does not automatically transform a USD 350,000 qualifying payment into a USD 400,000 investment.

The declared sale price, actual payments, applicable valuation confirmation and other legal requirements must be considered together.

**Important:** An inflated or inaccurate declared sale price is not a legitimate way to establish citizenship eligibility.

## 4. Check Banking Transfers and Foreign-Exchange Documentation

The way the purchase price is paid is critical.

Citizenship-related real estate transactions must comply with the applicable payment documentation and foreign-exchange procedures.

Depending on the transaction, the documentation may include:

- Bank transfer receipts.
- Evidence identifying the buyer and seller.
- Records of the purchase amount.
- Foreign-exchange purchase documentation, commonly referred to as a *Döviz Alım Belgesi* (DAB).
- Records linking payments to the relevant property transaction.
- Any additional documents requested by the competent authorities.

### Can you pay the seller in cash?

Investors should not assume that cash payments will satisfy the evidentiary requirements of a citizenship-related real estate purchase.

The payment structure must be checked in advance, and the relevant banking records should be preserved.

### Can someone else pay on your behalf?

Third-party payments require careful assessment.

A payment made by a relative, business partner or other third party may create documentation or compliance questions.

Before transferring money, investors should confirm the acceptable payment structure with their legal adviser and the relevant financial institution.

**What to do:** Establish the correct payment and foreign-exchange documentation process before transferring funds, rather than attempting to reconstruct the evidence afterward.

## 5. Check Mortgages, Restrictions and Property Legality

A property may have a market value above USD 400,000 and still present significant legal risks.

These risks can include:

- Existing mortgages or liens.
- Attachments or court-related restrictions.
- Unresolved construction or occupancy issues.
- Zoning or building compliance problems.
- Restrictions on ownership or transfer.
- Nationality-based property acquisition limitations.
- Disputes involving the seller or the property.

Some issues may be capable of resolution before completion. Others may make the proposed acquisition unsuitable.

Investors should distinguish between three separate questions:

**Can I legally buy this property?**

This concerns whether the acquisition and ownership transfer are permitted.

**Is this a financially sensible property investment?**

This concerns market value, rental prospects, maintenance, liquidity and resale risk.

**Can this transaction support a Turkish citizenship application?**

This concerns the specific citizenship investment requirements and supporting evidence.

A positive answer to one question does not automatically answer the others.

## 6. Understand the Three-Year Holding Requirement

For the real estate citizenship route, the qualifying acquisition generally requires a restriction against selling the property for at least three years.

The relevant commitment must be reflected in the official title deed procedures.

Investors should understand the legal consequences before purchasing.

### Can I rent the property during the three-year period?

Rental arrangements may be possible, subject to the property's legal status, applicable regulations and contractual restrictions.

However, ordinary residential rental and short-term tourist accommodation are governed by different requirements.

A property being eligible for citizenship does not automatically mean it can legally operate as an Airbnb or other short-term rental.

### Can I sell the property immediately after receiving citizenship?

Investors should not assume that citizenship approval removes the three-year holding commitment.

Any proposed sale or restructuring should be assessed against the applicable restriction and citizenship rules.

The holding period should be planned as part of the investment decision, not treated as an administrative formality.

## 7. Verify the Citizenship Conformity Process Before Completing the Purchase

The final question is whether the entire proposed transaction can satisfy the requirements for the relevant official conformity assessment.

The competent authorities evaluate the investment and its documentation.

An independent lawyer can identify potential issues, prepare the transaction correctly and coordinate the necessary documentation, but cannot guarantee the issuance of a conformity certificate or citizenship approval.

A pre-purchase assessment should therefore address:

- Whether the buyer is legally eligible to acquire the property.
- Whether the property and transaction structure are suitable for the citizenship route.
- Whether the required investment amount can be established.
- Whether the seller and ownership history create restrictions.
- Whether the banking and foreign-exchange records can be prepared correctly.
- Whether the three-year restriction can be properly registered.
- Whether the relevant conformity documentation can be obtained through the applicable process.

**The safest time to identify a citizenship eligibility problem is before the purchase becomes binding.**

## Real-World Scenarios: When a Property May Not Qualify

The following hypothetical examples illustrate common legal questions.

### Scenario A: The property costs USD 450,000, but the documents are incomplete

An investor agrees to purchase an apartment for USD 450,000.

However, the payment trail is unclear, and some of the required transaction documentation has not been properly arranged.

Despite the purchase amount exceeding the threshold, the citizenship-related documentation may be insufficient.

**Lesson:** Price alone is not enough.

### Scenario B: The apartment is newly built and has a ready title deed

A developer markets a newly completed apartment as suitable for Turkish citizenship.

The apartment has a title deed, but the buyer has not independently checked the seller's identity, the transaction history or the required investment documentation.

**Lesson:** A ready title deed does not replace a citizenship eligibility review.

### Scenario C: The investor already owns an apartment in Türkiye

An investor purchased an apartment several years ago and now wants to purchase another property to reach the citizenship investment threshold.

The earlier acquisition cannot automatically be counted merely because the investor still owns it.

The acquisition date, applicable rules at that time, title deed records, payment documentation, ownership history and any prior citizenship-related procedures must be examined.

**Lesson:** Existing property ownership requires an individual legal assessment before planning an additional purchase.

### Scenario D: The investor wants to purchase two apartments

An investor plans to purchase two apartments with a combined price above USD 400,000.

Multiple qualifying properties may be considered under the applicable framework, but the combined advertised price is not enough.

Each acquisition must be assessed, and the applicable documentation and timing requirements must be satisfied.

**Lesson:** The complete investment structure should be reviewed before either purchase.

## Documents to Request Before Buying a Property for Turkish Citizenship

Before making a binding commitment, request the following documents where available and applicable:

| Document | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Title deed (Tapu) | Confirms registered property details and ownership |
| Current land registry extract | Helps identify restrictions and annotations |
| Seller identification or company records | Establishes the seller's legal identity and authority |
| Ownership and transfer history | Helps identify citizenship-related transaction risks |
| Draft sale agreement | Allows review of purchase terms and payment obligations |
| Relevant valuation or investment amount documentation | Supports assessment of the qualifying investment |
| Banking and foreign-exchange documentation plan | Helps ensure payments are properly evidenced |
| Building and occupancy records | Helps identify property legality and construction-related risks |

Not every document will be available before the transaction, and some are generated during the official process.

The purpose of the pre-purchase review is to identify which records must be obtained, which procedures must be completed and which risks should be resolved before the investor commits.

## Should You Pay a Deposit Before the Legal Review?

If Turkish citizenship is a primary reason for buying the property, it is prudent to obtain independent legal advice before paying a non-refundable deposit.

Where a reservation or preliminary agreement is necessary, the investor should seek appropriate contractual protection.

Depending on the transaction, this may include conditions relating to:

- Satisfactory title deed and ownership checks.
- Citizenship investment documentation.
- Seller authority and property restrictions.
- The treatment of the deposit if material legal problems are identified.

The enforceability and appropriate form of these conditions must be assessed under Turkish law.

A reservation agreement should not be treated as proof that the property qualifies for citizenship.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is every property worth USD 400,000 eligible for Turkish citizenship?

No. The advertised or agreed price alone does not establish eligibility. The transaction must meet the applicable legal requirements, including qualifying investment value, ownership and transaction rules, documentation and the three-year holding commitment.

### Can I buy an apartment for USD 350,000 if its appraisal is USD 400,000?

A higher valuation does not automatically make a lower qualifying purchase payment eligible. The official sale price, actual payments, relevant investment amount confirmation and applicable rules must be assessed together.

### Does a new apartment automatically qualify for Turkish citizenship?

No. New construction and a ready title deed do not independently establish citizenship eligibility. The seller, ownership records, transaction structure, value and supporting documentation still require review.

### Can I use a property I purchased several years ago?

Possibly, depending on the acquisition date, rules applicable to that transaction, ownership history and required documents. Existing ownership does not automatically establish eligibility, and missing mandatory documentation may not be capable of being obtained retrospectively.

### Can I buy two apartments to reach the USD 400,000 requirement?

Multiple qualifying properties may be considered where the applicable requirements are met. The timing, values, ownership records and documentation for each acquisition require individual assessment.

### Can I rent out my citizenship investment property?

Rental arrangements may be possible, subject to applicable legal and contractual requirements. Short-term tourist rentals may require separate permits and compliance measures.

### Can I apply for citizenship immediately after buying?

Completion of the purchase does not itself establish eligibility for immediate citizenship approval. The required conformity and application procedures must be completed, and the competent authorities make the relevant decisions.

### Can a lawyer guarantee that a property qualifies?

No lawyer can guarantee the final decision of the competent authorities. Independent legal due diligence can identify risks, verify documentation and help structure the transaction in accordance with the applicable rules.
