# Turkish Citizenship to US E-2 Visa: The 3-Year Rule, Eligibility & Timeline (2026)

Turkish citizenship by investment can provide international investors with more than a second passport. For some entrepreneurs, it may also create a potential route to the United States through the E-2 Treaty Investor visa.

However, obtaining Turkish citizenship does not automatically make an investor eligible for an E-2 visa.

Under 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(E), individuals who acquired the relevant treaty-country nationality through financial investment and have not previously been granted E status must demonstrate at least three continuous years of domicile in that country at some point before applying. Turkish passport ownership alone does not satisfy that requirement.

This distinction is particularly important for investors from countries such as China and India who are exploring Turkish citizenship as part of a broader international business or relocation strategy.

This guide explains the relationship between Turkish citizenship and the US E-2 visa, the three-year domicile requirement, the investment obligations involved and the legal risks investors should evaluate before making financial commitments.

## Can Turkish Citizens Apply for a US E-2 Visa?

Turkey is an E-2 treaty country under the United States' treaty investor framework.

The E-2 classification allows qualifying nationals of treaty countries to seek temporary admission to the United States to develop and direct an enterprise in which they have invested, or are actively investing, a substantial amount of capital.

Turkish nationality can therefore satisfy the treaty nationality element of an E-2 application.

However, nationality is only one part of the eligibility assessment.

An applicant must also satisfy the applicable investment, business ownership or control, admissibility and other requirements.

Importantly, investors who obtained Turkish citizenship through a financial investment may face an additional domicile requirement.

The following distinctions are essential:

| Requirement | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Treaty nationality | The applicant must possess qualifying nationality of an E-2 treaty country. |
| Investment-acquired citizenship | Additional domicile rules may apply when nationality was acquired through financial investment. |
| US business investment | A separate qualifying investment in a US enterprise is generally required. |
| Business activity | The enterprise must meet the applicable E-2 requirements, including genuine commercial activity. |
| Visa approval | Possession of a Turkish passport does not guarantee approval. |

Investors should evaluate all these requirements together rather than treating Turkish citizenship as an automatic US immigration solution.

## The Three-Year Domicile Rule for Investment-Based Citizens

One of the most important developments affecting citizenship-by-investment planning is the three-year domicile rule introduced by section 5902(b) of Public Law 117-263, enacted on December 23, 2022.

The rule addresses certain individuals who acquired the nationality of an E-2 treaty country through financial investment.

Under 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(E), the requirement applies to applicants who acquired the relevant nationality through financial investment and have not previously been granted E status. They must establish a continuous period of at least three years of domicile in the treaty country, completed at some point before applying. The statute does not say that the period must fall immediately before the application.

This requirement is different from merely holding a passport for three years.

It is also different from making an investment in Turkey and waiting for the required period to expire.

The central issue is whether the applicant satisfies the applicable legal definition and evidentiary requirements for domicile.

### Does the Rule Apply to Every Turkish Citizen?

No.

The special rule concerns certain nationality acquisitions through financial investment.

It should not be presented as a universal three-year waiting period for all Turkish citizens.

Individuals who obtained Turkish nationality by birth, descent, marriage or other legal routes may be subject to different considerations.

The statutory qualification concerning people previously granted E status must be evaluated individually. Acquiring a passport before December 23, 2022 is not, by itself, an exemption stated in the amendment.

The circumstances of nationality acquisition therefore matter.

### Does the Three-Year Period Begin When the Turkish Passport Is Issued?

Not necessarily.

Investors should not assume that the legal requirement always begins on the date their Turkish passport is issued.

The relevant US statutory wording and applicable guidance must be examined to determine how qualifying domicile periods are assessed.

A person's actual residence and domicile history may be legally relevant. The statute does not expressly say that the entire qualifying period must follow nationality acquisition; that wording should not be treated as a guarantee that a particular pre-naturalization period will be accepted.

For example, an investor who genuinely established domicile in Turkey before acquiring Turkish nationality may present a different factual situation from someone who acquired citizenship while living entirely abroad.

Whether a particular period qualifies requires individual legal analysis.

A simplified rule such as "receive your passport, wait three years and apply" can therefore be misleading.

## What Does Domicile in Turkey Mean?

Domicile generally concerns a person's principal home and intention to maintain it. Section 5902(b) does not supply a detailed E-visa domicile definition or evidence checklist, so this general explanation is not a definitive test for an individual application.

It should not be confused automatically with citizenship, immigration permission, property ownership or occasional physical presence.

An investor who purchases an apartment in Istanbul but continues to live and work permanently in another country should not assume that the apartment alone establishes Turkish domicile.

For discussion with US immigration counsel, potentially relevant evidence may include the following. This is an indicative planning list, not an official E-2 domicile checklist:

- Residence history and immigration records.
- Housing arrangements and the location of the principal home.
- Employment or business activities.
- Tax and financial documentation.
- Family and personal connections.
- Children's education, where applicable.
- Other records showing the nature and continuity of a person's life in Turkey.

No single document necessarily proves domicile.

The overall factual circumstances and applicable US legal standards are important.

Investors considering this pathway should discuss domicile requirements with qualified US immigration counsel before relying on a particular residence strategy.

## A Realistic Timeline: From Turkish Citizenship to US E-2 Planning

The Turkish citizenship and US E-2 processes involve separate legal stages.

Their timing should not be confused.

The following timeline illustrates a possible planning sequence rather than a guaranteed processing schedule.

### Stage 1 — Assess Turkish Citizenship Eligibility

An investor first determines whether they qualify for Turkish citizenship through an available investment route. Our [Turkish citizenship by investment legal services](/en/turkish-citizenship-by-investment-lawyer/) address the Turkish side of this assessment.

Under the Turkish real estate investment route, the applicable qualifying threshold is generally USD 400,000, subject to the relevant legal requirements.

A qualifying purchase must satisfy more than a headline price. Review the [Turkish citizenship investment requirements](/en/blog/turkish-citizenship-by-real-estate-investment-2025-complete-guide/).

Depending on the transaction, legal checks may include:

- Ownership and title deed status.
- Eligibility under citizenship regulations.
- Official valuation requirements.
- Banking and foreign exchange documentation.
- Applicable restrictions on the property.
- The required undertaking concerning disposal of the property.

Independent legal due diligence before purchase is particularly important. Our guide explains how to [verify property eligibility before buying](/en/blog/how-to-check-property-eligibility-turkish-citizenship/).

### Stage 2 — Complete the Turkish Citizenship Process

Following a qualifying investment, the investor completes the required documentation and citizenship application procedures.

The timeline varies depending on the transaction, documentation, administrative review and individual circumstances.

Investors should not rely on a guaranteed processing period.

Family members may be eligible for inclusion under the applicable Turkish citizenship rules.

However, family eligibility should be assessed individually.

### Stage 3 — Evaluate and Document Domicile

For investors subject to the US three-year domicile requirement, this stage may be central to future E-2 eligibility.

A genuine domicile strategy involves more than retaining ownership of Turkish property.

The investor's actual living arrangements, personal circumstances and supporting evidence may be relevant.

The legal assessment must also consider whether any prior period of domicile qualifies.

A timeline should therefore be built around the applicant's facts, not simply around the passport issuance date.

### Stage 4 — Plan a Separate US Business Investment

The E-2 classification requires an investment that independently meets US immigration requirements.

The Turkish citizenship investment and US E-2 business investment are not interchangeable.

An investor should evaluate the proposed US business, lawful source and at-risk commitment of funds, ownership or operational control, commercial viability and applicable visa requirements. The enterprise must be real and operating and must not be marginal under the E-2 standard. The applicant must intend to depart when E status ends.

There is no universal statutory minimum investment amount that guarantees E-2 eligibility.

The substantiality of an investment is assessed under the relevant legal framework and circumstances.

### Stage 5 — Apply for the US E-2 Visa

Once the relevant requirements have been met, the investor may pursue the appropriate E-2 application process with qualified US immigration counsel.

The applicable procedure depends on the applicant's circumstances. Consular visa issuance and an eligible change of status through USCIS are different procedures; a change of status in the United States does not itself issue a visa for future travel.

Approval remains discretionary and subject to all applicable requirements.

Turkish citizenship does not guarantee issuance of a US visa.

## Turkish Citizenship Investment vs US E-2 Business Investment

A common misunderstanding is that purchasing USD 400,000 worth of Turkish real estate automatically satisfies the US E-2 investment requirement.

It does not.

The two investments serve different legal purposes. The comparison of [Turkish citizenship investment options](/en/blog/real-estate-vs-bank-deposit-vs-bes-for-turkish-citizenship-a-legal-and-strategic-comparison-2026/) addresses the Turkish investment stage.

| Feature | Turkish Citizenship by Investment | US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa |
|---|---|---|
| Primary purpose | Qualifying investment supporting Turkish citizenship | Temporary US immigration classification connected to a qualifying enterprise |
| Typical investment | Qualifying Turkish real estate or another approved route | Investment in a qualifying US business |
| Investment threshold | Generally USD 400,000 for the real estate route | No universal fixed statutory minimum |
| Key legal consideration | Compliance with Turkish citizenship investment requirements | Treaty nationality, qualifying investment and applicable immigration requirements |
| Outcome | Potential acquisition of Turkish citizenship | Potential temporary US E-2 status or visa |
| Automatic approval | No | No |

The Turkish property purchase does not substitute for the required US enterprise investment.

An investor considering both stages should therefore prepare a financial plan that accounts for separate capital requirements, legal expenses, operating costs and potential delays.

## Two Different Three-Year Rules Investors Must Not Confuse

The Turkish citizenship investment program and the US E-2 framework may both involve three-year periods.

However, they are legally different.

### Turkey's Real Estate Holding Requirement

The Turkish real estate citizenship route generally involves a requirement not to sell the qualifying property for a specified three-year period.

The exact legal effect of the undertaking and any later transaction should be assessed under Turkish law. See our guide to [selling citizenship investment property after three years](/en/blog/turkish-citizenship-property-sale-after-3-years-how-to-sell-your-property-investment-in-turkey/).

### The US E-2 Domicile Requirement

The US requirement concerns qualifying domicile in the treaty country for certain individuals who acquired nationality through financial investment.

Holding Turkish property for three years does not automatically establish domicile.

Similarly, satisfying the Turkish property holding requirement does not independently establish US E-2 eligibility.

An investor could comply with the Turkish investment holding obligation while failing to satisfy the applicable US domicile requirement.

These rules should be evaluated separately before making an investment decision.

## Can Chinese Investors Use Turkish Citizenship for the E-2 Visa?

For some Chinese investors, Turkish citizenship may form part of a broader international mobility strategy.

Mainland China does not have E-2 treaty coverage on the State Department list, whereas Turkey does. The list's separate China (Taiwan) entry must not be treated as coverage for mainland Chinese nationality.

Consequently, acquiring qualifying Turkish nationality may create a potential treaty nationality basis for an E-2 application.

However, several important limitations apply.

First, the applicant must satisfy the relevant US requirements, including the special domicile rule where applicable.

Second, obtaining Turkish citizenship does not automatically establish eligibility for a US visa.

Third, Chinese nationality law must be considered independently.

Article 3 of China's Nationality Law does not recognize dual nationality for Chinese nationals. Under Article 9, a Chinese national who has settled abroad and voluntarily acquires foreign nationality loses Chinese nationality automatically; the statutory conditions and the individual's circumstances require Chinese-law advice.

The investor should obtain advice concerning both Turkish and Chinese nationality consequences before proceeding.

The commercial benefits of an additional passport should never be presented without explaining the potential nationality-law risks. Our guide to [Turkish citizenship for Chinese investors](/en/blog/turkish-citizenship-for-chinese-citizens-in-2026-documents-hukou-apostille-family-guide/) covers Turkish application documents and family issues.

## What About Indian Investors?

Indian investors may also investigate Turkish citizenship when evaluating international business and relocation options.

However, the acquisition of another nationality can have important consequences under Indian nationality law.

Under section 9 of India's Citizenship Act 1955, voluntary acquisition of another citizenship generally terminates Indian citizenship, subject to the Act's provisions. Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI) is not Indian citizenship.

An investor should therefore not assume that acquiring Turkish citizenship allows unrestricted retention of Indian nationality.

In addition, US E-2 eligibility must be independently evaluated.

The Turkish citizenship investment should make sense in its own right rather than being justified solely by an assumed future US visa outcome.

## Who Might Consider This Pathway?

Turkish citizenship followed by potential E-2 eligibility may be relevant to certain investors who:

- Already have a legitimate interest in Turkish citizenship.
- Want to establish meaningful personal or business ties with Turkey.
- Are prepared to evaluate the domicile requirements carefully.
- Have separate capital available for a potential US business investment.
- Understand that US visa eligibility is not guaranteed.
- Are considering a long-term rather than immediate relocation strategy.

However, this pathway may not be appropriate for everyone.

For example, an investor who needs to relocate to the United States immediately may find that the domicile requirements and other legal conditions make this strategy unsuitable.

Similarly, an investor who intends to acquire a Turkish passport without establishing genuine ties to Turkey should not assume that the passport alone provides a practical E-2 solution.

## Common Mistakes Investors Should Avoid

### Mistake 1 — Assuming a Turkish Passport Guarantees an E-2 Visa

Turkish nationality is only one potential component of eligibility.

The investor must satisfy all relevant US immigration requirements.

### Mistake 2 — Confusing Property Ownership With Domicile

Buying property in Turkey does not automatically establish that Turkey is the investor's domicile.

### Mistake 3 — Treating the Turkish Property as the US Business Investment

The investments are legally separate.

### Mistake 4 — Ignoring Existing Nationality Rules

Chinese and Indian investors should carefully consider the consequences of acquiring another nationality.

### Mistake 5 — Assuming Three Years of Passport Ownership Is Sufficient

The relevant rule concerns qualifying domicile, not simply the age of the passport.

### Mistake 6 — Making Financial Commitments Before Legal Review

Investors should evaluate both Turkish citizenship eligibility and potential US immigration limitations before committing substantial capital.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I apply for an E-2 visa immediately after obtaining Turkish citizenship?

Not necessarily. Individuals who acquired treaty-country nationality through financial investment may be subject to a three-year continuous domicile requirement, alongside all other E-2 eligibility rules. Individual circumstances and applicable exceptions must be assessed.

### Does the three-year domicile rule apply to every Turkish citizen?

No. The special rule concerns certain forms of investment-acquired nationality. It should not be treated as a universal requirement for all Turkish citizens.

### Must I wait three years after receiving my Turkish passport?

Not necessarily. The relevant law addresses qualifying domicile, and the applicant's actual domicile history may matter. The applicable period should be assessed under current US law and guidance.

### Does owning an apartment in Istanbul prove domicile?

No. Property ownership alone does not necessarily establish domicile. Actual living arrangements, personal ties and other relevant evidence may be important.

### Does my USD 400,000 Turkish property count as the E-2 investment?

No. The Turkish citizenship investment and the qualifying US business investment serve different legal purposes.

### Can Chinese citizens use this pathway?

Potentially, but Turkish nationality, applicable US domicile requirements, US business investment conditions and Chinese nationality law must all be considered.

### Can Indian citizens use this pathway?

Potentially, subject to Turkish nationality acquisition, applicable US E-2 requirements and the consequences of Indian nationality law.

### Does an E-2 visa lead directly to a green card?

No. E-2 is a temporary nonimmigrant classification and does not itself provide an automatic route to US permanent residence.

### Can my family accompany me under E-2?

Qualifying spouses and unmarried children under 21 may be eligible for derivative E classification, subject to the applicable rules and individual circumstances.

### Can I sell my Turkish citizenship property after three years?

The Turkish investment-related holding requirements must be assessed under Turkish law. Completing the required period does not automatically establish US E-2 eligibility or domicile.
