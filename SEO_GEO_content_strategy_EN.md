# IGA Law Firm — SEO and GEO Content Strategy

**Research date:** 28 September 2026  
**Scope:** Publicly visible igalawfirm.com pages, service pages and blog archive; Turkish keyword clusters; blog outlines; SEO and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) requirements.

> **Data limitation:** This is qualitative research based on content visible through search. I did not have access to Search Console, Google Ads Keyword Planner or paid SEO tools, so search volumes, difficulty scores and current rankings have not been verified. The keywords below are candidate targets prioritized by search intent and service relevance, not claims about volume. Attorneys should verify legal thresholds and claims against official sources before publication.

## 1. Site review and content opportunities

IGA Law Firm is positioned around Turkish citizenship by investment, immigration law and legal advice for international investors. The visible practice areas include citizenship, work permits, corporate and commercial law, legal consultancy, litigation, residence permits, real estate due diligence, and documentation/apostille services. The blog currently concentrates on citizenship investment routes, real estate, bank deposits, BES/funds and documents for Chinese applicants.

### Findings

- The citizenship section already contains several similar “2026 guides,” investment-route comparisons and property articles. New content should address distinct, narrower questions instead of competing with existing pages. Consolidate posts targeting the same intent or clearly differentiate their scope.
- Content depth is comparatively limited for work and residence permits, company formation, commercial contracts, property due diligence and litigation services.
- The English blog appears more visible and current. Review whether Turkish and Chinese pages and articles provide comparable coverage. Each language should be edited for its own search intent rather than published as a mechanical copy.
- The English homepage lists a Beşiktaş/Bebek office address, while the Turkish homepage lists Şişli/Esentepe. Team names and roles also appear inconsistent. Standardize the address, team, phone, WhatsApp/WeChat, and “since 2018” and “600+ families” claims across all languages and directories.
- Claims involving a year, processing time, tax advantage or investment return require legal review. Case examples such as “approval in 6 months” must not imply a universal outcome or guarantee.

## 4. SEO requirements

### On-page

- Choose one search intent and one primary target keyword per URL. Use it naturally in the title, H1, introduction and meta description.
- Write a unique, descriptive SEO title and a meta description that matches the reader’s intent; Google may rewrite them. Use one H1, a logical H2/H3 hierarchy, a descriptive URL slug and contextual image alt text.
- If a year appears in the title, the content must actually be current for that year. Prefer keeping the URL and updating the content and update note when the year changes.
- Add 3–6 contextual internal links per article: the relevant service page, topic guide and related supporting articles. Anchor text should describe the destination.
- Link to official legislation and agency sources. Distinguish official rules from the firm’s interpretation and case experience.
- Use original diagrams/images, useful captions, compressed files and accessible alt text. Do not hide essential information inside images.
- Include a restrained CTA to the relevant legal service on informational pages.

### Technical and multilingual SEO

- Use Search Console to check indexing, canonical URLs, sitemap, robots directives, mobile usability and Core Web Vitals. This review could not verify robots.txt or the sitemap; that does not mean they are absent.
- Implement correct `hreflang`, self-referencing canonicals and reciprocal language alternatives for Turkish, English and Chinese pages. Edit every language for local search intent.
- Set an indexing/canonical policy for blog pagination, tag/category URLs and duplicate titles.
- Consider Organization/LegalService, Person, BlogPosting/Article and Breadcrumb structured data only when it reflects visible, verifiable information. Schema does not guarantee rich results or GEO visibility.
- Keep NAP (business name, address and phone) consistent across the website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places and social profiles. Do not add keywords to the business name.
- Track language, page group, query, impressions/clicks and contact conversions in Search Console and analytics. Reprioritize the pool once search volume and ranking data are available.

## 5. GEO / requirements for content cited in AI answers

GEO here means Generative Engine Optimization. Google’s official guidance says foundational SEO still applies to AI search visibility: pages should be crawlable and eligible for indexing and a search snippet, with unique and reliable content. Google says a special AI schema or `llms.txt`, an AI-specific writing formula, and mandatory micro-chunking are not required. The editorial standards below support accurate answers and readability; they do not guarantee visibility.

### Requirements for every article

1. **Answer directly:** Open with a concise paragraph that answers the main question and states important conditions and exceptions.
2. **Use explicit question headings:** Ask “Who can apply?” or “Which documents are required?” in H2s and answer immediately below.
3. **Use a predictable structure:** Definition → eligibility/conditions → steps → documents → refusal/delay reasons → exceptions → FAQ.
4. **State date and jurisdiction:** Show the last legal review date, that the guide concerns Turkish law, the relevant law/agency and a note where rules can change. Change the date only after a real review.
5. **Use authoritative sources:** Prioritize legislation/Official Gazette, ministries and regulators. Separate firm commentary from official text; do not rely on another blog as the sole legal authority.
6. **Show the author and reviewer:** Identify the actual author and reviewing attorney, with a verifiable professional profile. Do not present a translator as the legal reviewer.
7. **Make content readable by people and machines:** Present process tables, checklists and steps as HTML text; do not bury essential information in a PDF or image.
8. **Explain uncertainty:** Avoid universal promises about timing or outcomes. Explain that processing time and document requests may vary by application location, family circumstances, issuing country and current practice.
9. **Use original experience responsibly:** Obtain permission and anonymize case studies. For success, timing or return claims, state the date, scope and method; do not imply a guarantee.
10. **Keep language versions consistent:** Turkish, English and Chinese versions should match in legal meaning, dates, thresholds and caveats, with separate editorial and legal review in each language.
11. **Provide safe contact paths:** State services and credentials clearly. Check privacy and KVKK compliance for document submission flows.
12. **Maintain a review schedule:** Review citizenship, residence, work, tax and fee articles annually or after a legal change; record material updates.

### GEO article template

```text
H1: One clear topic/question + target phrase
Short answer: One paragraph answering the question, with scope and key condition
Last legal review: [date] | Reviewer: [attorney profile]
Who is this guide for? [reader + jurisdiction]
H2: Definition / who may apply?
H2: Requirements and current official basis
H2: Step-by-step process
H2: Documents / checklist
H2: Reasons for refusal or delay
H2: Exceptions and when to consult a lawyer
H2: Frequently asked questions — direct answers
Sources: official links + access/update date
Author / legal reviewer + relevant profiles
Internal links to relevant services and guides
```

## 6. Recommended implementation order

1. **Trust and accuracy:** Standardize address, team, services, contact details and experience claims across languages. Have attorneys review tax benefits, current thresholds and processing-time claims.
2. **Existing content:** Use Search Console queries to differentiate the citizenship pillar, property, bank/BES and Chinese-applicant pages. Consolidate overlapping articles or separate their scope and link them clearly.
3. **First new articles:** Documents, residence permits, work permits and property legal due diligence; then company formation, apostille and commercial contracts.
4. **Technical/multilingual:** Verify NAP, hreflang/canonical, author/reviewer details and structured data.
5. **Measurement:** Track impressions/clicks by query/page in Search Console and form, call and WhatsApp-click conversions. Reprioritize after an initial 8–12 weeks of baseline data.
